### 2. GNSS Fundamentals
**Global Navigation Satellite Systems** include:
- **GPS** (USA)
- **GLONASS** (Russia)
- **Galileo** (EU)
- **BeiDou** (China)
- **NavIC** (India, regional)

Each constellation provides positioning, navigation, and timing (PNT).

### 3. Why Augmentation?
Standard GNSS accuracy:
- **Civilian GPS** — 3–5 meters.
- **Military GPS** — Sub-meter (encrypted).
- **Augmented** — 1 cm precision.

Augmentation corrects for:
- **Ionospheric delay** — Signal distortion from the atmosphere.
- **Satellite clock errors** — Slight drifts.
- **Ephemeris errors** — Orbital prediction inaccuracies.
- **Multipath** — Signal reflection off buildings/terrain.

### 4. Differential GNSS (DGNSS) & DNPT
**Differential Network Positioning Techniques (DNPT)** use a network of fixed ground stations that:
1. Know their exact positions.
2. Receive GNSS signals.
3. Calculate the error.
4. Broadcast corrections to nearby users.

Result: centimeter-level accuracy over a region.

### 5. Core Ground Stations
Ground stations for GNSS augmentation need:
- **Survey-grade receivers** — Multi-constellation, multi-frequency.
- **Stable monumentation** — Rock-solid mounting.
- **Communications** — Data links to users.
- **Power and security** — Redundancy and protection.
- **Network design** — Coverage geometry optimized.

### 6. FALCON EYE Integration
**FALCON EYE** is a wide-area persistent surveillance system. In your notes, it appears as a potential integration with core ground architecture.

Integration value:
- **Continuous monitoring** — Persistent coverage of an area.
- **Integration with GNSS** — Precision location of detected objects.
- **Command support** — Real-time situational awareness.
