# Lesson 3.5 — PPP Models for the Nigerian Space Industry

**Module:** M3 — Space Economy, Capacity Building & Human Capital

### 2. What Is a PPP?
A Public-Private Partnership is a long-term contractual arrangement where:
- **Government** provides: licensing, spectrum, orbital slots, anchor contracts, regulatory support.
- **Private partner** provides: capital, technical operations, commercial sales, management.
- **Both share** risk and reward.

### 3. PPP Variants
- **BOT (Build-Operate-Transfer)** — Partner builds and operates, then transfers to government.
- **BOO (Build-Own-Operate)** — Partner retains permanent ownership.
- **DBFOM (Design-Build-Finance-Operate-Maintain)** — Full lifecycle partner involvement.
- **Concession** — Government retains ownership, partner operates under license.

### 4. Standardization Framework for Nigeria
For the Nigerian space industry, a standardized PPP framework should include:
1. **Eligibility criteria** — Clear requirements for private partners.
2. **Risk allocation matrix** — Who bears what risk (technical, financial, political).
3. **Performance metrics** — Measurable KPIs tied to payments.
4. **Dispute resolution** — Clear arbitration mechanisms.
5. **Exit clauses** — Government's right to buy out partner after a set period.
6. **Sovereignty safeguards** — National security data protections.

### 5. Risks & Rewards

| Risk | Reward |
| :--- | :--- |
| Contract complexity | Access to private capital |
| Profit-sharing tensions | Technical expertise |
| Political interference | Faster delivery |
| Partner default | Reduced treasury burden |

### 6. Case Studies
- **Brazil** — PPP for the SGDC defense/communications satellite.
- **Turkey** — Public-private partnership for Göktürk EO satellites.
- **UAE** — Mixed public-private model for Mohammed bin Rashid Space Centre.
